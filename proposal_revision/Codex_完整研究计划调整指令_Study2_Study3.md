## 7.2 Number of moves

Construct:

- moves before Associate Professor;
- moves after Associate Professor;
- moves per observed career year;
- early-career moves;
- time since last move.

Why:

Meaning:

## 7.3 Internal versus external promotion

Where data permit, classify:

- `INTERNAL_PROMOTION`
- `EXTERNAL_PROMOTION`
- `PROMOTION_WITH_UNRESOLVED_MOVE_STATUS`

Why:

- promotion within the same institution and promotion accompanied by an institutional move are sociologically different processes.

Question answered:

- whether career advancement depends on organizational loyalty or movement across institutional boundaries.

## 7.4 Institutional prestige

Do not use raw annual ranks as the only measure.

Preserve separate ranking systems:

- THE;
- QS;
- ARWU.

For institution j in year t, compute an annual within-ranking percentile:

`prestige_percentile_jt = (N_t - rank_jt) / (N_t - 1)`

Higher values indicate greater prestige.

For a move in year t, use the median prestige percentile from t-3 to t-1 for both origin and destination where available.

Construct:

- origin prestige percentile;
- destination prestige percentile;
- prestige change;
- upward move;
- lateral move;
- downward move;
- origin prestige tier;
- destination prestige tier;
- tier change;
- ranking coverage status;
- number of ranking systems available.

Primary move classification:

- upward: destination minus origin ≥ 10 percentile points;
- lateral: absolute difference < 10 points;
- downward: destination minus origin ≤ -10 points.

Use 5- and 15-point thresholds as sensitivity analyses.

Why:

- annual percentile adjusts for changes in ranking coverage;
- three-year medians reduce one-year noise;
- tiers prevent small ranking fluctuations from being treated as substantive career transitions.

Meaning:

- organizational-status mobility.

Question answered:

- whether moves into higher-status institutions are associated with promotion and whether those associations differ by gender.

Do not average raw THE, QS, and ARWU ranks.

## 7.5 Broad institutional prestige tiers

Construct broad states for sequence analysis.

Suggested states:

- `TIER_1`
- `TIER_2`
- `TIER_3`
- `TIER_4`
- `UNRANKED`
- `RESEARCH_INSTITUTE`
- `OVERSEAS_UNCLASSIFIED`
- `NONACADEMIC`
- `NOT_OBSERVED`

Use percentile cut points or a documented structural classification.

Why:

- sequence analysis requires a limited, interpretable state alphabet;

Why:

Meaning:

# 8. Study 2 analytical design

Study 2 should combine two complementary designs.

## 8.1 Event-history analysis

Unit:

- researcher-year.

Primary outcome:

- first Associate Professor transition in year t.

Time-varying predictors:

- cumulative move count through t-1;
- move in t-1;
- time since last move;
- upward/lateral/downward move history;
- prestige change;
- institution-size change;
- institutional stability;
- gender interactions.

Primary models:

- discrete-time complementary log-log;
- discrete-time logit as comparison;
- Cox model as robustness.

Use flexible baseline hazard based on time since PhD.

Interpretation:

- time-ordered mobility-career associations, not causal effects.

## 8.2 Multichannel sequence analysis

Construct career-year sequences aligned from PhD.

Primary window:

- career years 0–15.

Channels:

1. rank trajectory;
2. institutional prestige-tier trajectory;
3. institutional mobility trajectory;
4. optional institution-size trajectory as sensitivity or external descriptor.

Do not combine all dimensions into an enormous joint state.

Compute channel-specific distances and then combine standardized distance matrices.

Methods:

- Optimal Matching;
- transition-rate substitution costs;
- Dynamic Hamming where suitable;
- PAM/k-medoids as primary clustering;
- Ward as comparison;
- medoids and representative sequences;
- cluster stability;
- silhouette;
- bootstrap or resampling validation.

Gender must not be included as a clustering feature.

Name clusters from observed pathway characteristics before inspecting gender proportions.

Possible descriptive labels must not be imposed in advance.

Examples only:

- stable institutional insiders;
- upward mobile climbers;
- repeated lateral movers;
- mobile without upward return;
- late upward movers.

## 8.3 Equal-destination analysis

Among researchers who reach the same final rank, compare:

- number of moves;
- upward-move frequency;
- total prestige gain;
- institution-size change;
- pathway-cluster membership;
- time to promotion;
- gender composition.

Purpose:

- test whether equal destinations conceal unequal pathways.

## 10.4 Funding

Separate access from funding intensity.

Use a two-part structure:

1. KAKEN access;
2. funding intensity conditional on positive funding.

Construct:

- direct funding;
- `log_positive_direct_funding`;
- field-year standardized funding;
- full project exposure;
- equal fractional exposure;
- role-weighted fractional exposure.

Unlinked members must remain in fractional-allocation denominators.

Why:

- zero-inflated log funding can become almost identical to KAKEN access;
- the two-part specification separates entry into funding from the amount received among participants.

## 10.5 Project count

Construct annual and rolling project counts.

Separate:

- all projects;
- PI projects;
- Co-I projects;
- active projects.

Why:

- number of simultaneous projects represents resource breadth and workload, not only funding amount.

## 10.6 KAKEN-based collaboration networks

Construct a researcher-project bipartite network and annual or rolling researcher-researcher projections.

Main measures:

- degree;
- weighted degree;
- PI outdegree;
- PI indegree.

Primary main-model choice:

- degree;
- PI outdegree;
- PI indegree.

Use weighted degree as a robustness replacement for degree.

Do not include degree and weighted degree together in the main model.

Interpretation:

- degree: number of distinct formal project collaborators;
- weighted degree: repeated or multiple project ties;
- PI outdegree: leadership reach through PI-led projects;
- PI indegree: inclusion in projects led by others.

Do not call this a co-authorship network.

## 10.7 Time ordering

For promotion in year t:

Primary:

- t-1;
- past3, defined as t-3 through t-1.

Robustness:

- past4;
- past5;
- cumulative prior exposure.

Why:

- resources must precede the promotion year;
- rolling windows capture sustained exposure;
- lagging improves temporal ordering but does not identify causality.
